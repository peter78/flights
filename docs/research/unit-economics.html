Unit Economics

What it costs to serve a search, what each monetization model is worth per search, and where the real swing variables live. Numbers are 2026 point estimates with wide bands — treat them as directional, not budgetable.

Assumptions, stated up front

  • Average air ticket: $400 round-trip, all-in.
  • Average EU rail leg: €60; average EU bus leg: €30; average attraction: $80.
  • Cache hit rate on popular routes: 70–90% per the design in Caching & Indexing.
  • Search→book click-through conversion: 2–5% for air metasearch, 3–7% for rail, 2–5% for attractions. These are the most uncertain numbers in the whole analysis.
  • Pricing reflects late-2025 / early-2026 list rates for Claude Haiku 4.5, Bright Data residential proxies, Mapbox, Google Maps Platform, managed Postgres / Redis, Duffel.
  • Confidence bands on every number are roughly ±30–50%. The verdict survives 2× uncertainty on any single line.
Caveat These are the right kind of numbers to make architectural and sequencing decisions on. They are not the right kind of numbers to forecast a P&L on. Past about 18 months out, every line is dominated by pricing trajectory of LLMs and proxies — both still falling fast.

  • At 80% cache hit rate, steady-state amortized costs are ~20–25% of the cold-cache numbers above.

Subscription LTV detail

  • ARPU: $79/yr (Going low-tier baseline; high-tier $199 lifts blended ARPU into the $90–120 range only with a content treadmill).
  • COGS per subscriber per year: ~$0.50–$1 search compute + $1–3 email/alerts + $2–10 curation if AI-augmented + $3–5 payment processing ≈ $10–20/yr.
  • Gross margin: 75–85%.
  • Churn: 25–35% annual is normal for travel-deal subs; that's a ~3–4 year LTV multiplier on ARPU.
  • Resulting LTV: $200–$320. Realistic blended CAC: $20–$60. LTV/CAC ~5–10× if the funnel converts.
Direction Subscription is the only model where LTV/CAC clearly exceeds 5× with reasonable assumptions. Affiliate is positive-contribution but doesn't compound; OTA can't amortize overhead until scale that takes years. The B2B angle is a separate parallel motion with different sales mechanics.

The sensitivities that actually matter

  1. Bright Data spend. At <100k searches/mo it's a fixed tax that breaks unit economics; at 1M+ it goes variable and linear. Plan: subscription model carries the $500/mo floor early; bypassing into Duffel for popular routes later cuts both fixed and variable.
  2. Click-to-book conversion. 2% vs 5% is a 2.5× revenue swing. Dominated by deep-link handoff quality (parameter passing, currency, dates surviving) — not search engineering.
  3. Cache hit rate. 70% vs 90% changes variable cost by ~3×. The architecture is already designed for this; the open question is whether top-route concentration in real traffic matches the design assumption.
  4. Attach rate of ground + attractions. Multi-stop trip composition is the moat and the revenue multiplier. Pure aviation affiliate is a low-margin business; bundling lifts blended take rate ~2× (see Ground Transit and Places & POI).
  5. LLM pricing trajectory. Haiku has been on a steady downward path. If that continues, NLU effectively rounds to zero. Do not over-engineer for LLM cost reduction in v1.

What changes the math, in scenarios

Pessimistic (everything goes wrong)

  • Cache hit rate 50%, conversion 1.5%, Bright Data renegotiation pushes proxy cost +50%.
  • Per-search variable cost at 100k/mo doubles to ~$0.02. Revenue/search at $0.15. CM still positive at ~$13k/mo, but payback on CAC becomes 4–6 bookings per user instead of 2–3.

Base case

  • Numbers as above. At 100k searches/mo, blended affiliate + 5% subscription attach: ~$60–90k monthly contribution. Enough to fund one engineer + minimal marketing.

Optimistic (multi-stop attach works)

  • Cache hit 85%, multi-stop trip planner doubles ground + attraction attach, subscription attach 8%.
  • Blended revenue/search $1.10. Variable $0.015. CM/search $1.08 × 100k = ~$108k/mo.
  • Subscription LTV approaches $400 with content + alert retention loops; this is the path to genuine venture-scale economics.

Where the headline OTA economics sit, for reference

If we ever do graduate to full reseller (Model 2), per-booking economics look roughly:

  • $400 ticket × 12% blended take rate = $48 gross
  • Less payment processing 2.5%: $38 net
  • Less refund-reserve ~3% of GMV: $26–30 net
  • Less customer service per booking: $2–5
  • Net contribution: $20–$25 per booking

That's attractive only after enough volume to amortize the $50–500k bonding + accreditation + working-capital drag. Don't model it inside v1.

CAC, the variable I'm least confident about

  • Travel direct-to-consumer blended CAC ranges $20–$60 across paid + organic in 2025/2026 data.
  • Subscription products skew lower if a free product feeds the funnel (which is exactly the affiliate-then-subscription setup recommended in Monetization).
  • Organic SEO around long-tail intermodal queries ("munich to crete cheap with train") is realistically achievable because incumbents don't rank for them — this is the cheapest acquisition channel and the one that scales with the product itself.
  • Paid search against branded competitors is expensive and a money pit until LTV is proven.

Putting it together — the directional verdict

  • Search itself is cheap. Cents per search, not dollars. The cost story is the Bright Data floor early and proxy linearity at scale, not LLM tokens or DB writes.
  • Affiliate is the day-1 unlock and pays for itself instantly — but it does not compound. Treat it as a funding mechanism for the engine, not an end state.
  • Subscription is the natural high-CM business. The price_observations moat designed in Caching compounds month-over-month; multi-stop trip composition is what makes the deal feed differentiated from a Going clone.
  • B2B service-fee licensing is a real adjacent market worth a 1-engineer pilot at month 6–9. Different sales motion, different conversion economics, but same engine.
  • OTA is a future option. Sub-1M-booking volume can't amortize bonding + accreditation. Revisit only after subscription LTV > $300 and >100k monthly actives.
The bet Build the engine. Earn affiliate from day one to cover infrastructure. Layer subscription on top of price_observations at month ~6. Pilot B2B in parallel at month ~9. Don't touch OTA accreditation until the demand shape is so clear it would be malpractice not to.

Open questions to revisit with real data

  • What is our actual cache hit rate once we have 30 days of real traffic? Design assumes 70–90% but the long tail of natural-language queries may not concentrate as much as route-based searches do.
  • What's the click-to-book conversion delta between a "Protected intermodal" badged result and a generic one? If protected journeys convert 2× better, that changes ranker weights and affiliate priorities.
  • How much does the multi-stop trip planner lift blended revenue per session? This is the single biggest moat-or-not question.
  • Are subscribers retaining at >65% annually? Anything lower kneecaps the LTV story.
  • Does B2B at $50–100 / seat / month actually clear? Or does the AI-planning category get cannibalised by Salesforce Concur / SAP Travel before we build it?

How this connects to the engine

  • Caching & Indexing — cache hit rate is the single biggest variable-cost lever. The price_observations table is the subscription moat.
  • Data & Providers — the path off Bright Data and onto Duffel/Amadeus is what flips the cost structure from "floor + small variable" to "no floor + small variable".
  • NLU & LLMs — Haiku pricing trajectory is the optimistic-scenario driver; prompt caching is already designed in.
  • Monetization — per-model take rates, accreditation overhead, and the legal posture of each path.
  • Evals — production telemetry (Tier 3) measures the actual cache hit rate, conversion, and stability metrics that this analysis hinges on.
  • Roadmap — sequence and decision checkpoints are calibrated to these economics.
